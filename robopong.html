RoboPong

Real-time ping pong playing robotic arm

Overview

For my senior capstone project, my team and I decided to build a robotic arm that can play ping pong against an opponent in real time. We submitted our idea to the UW-Madison Borgen design competition where we won funding. The project is still ongoing but I'm happy with the progress during our first semester.

Ball Tracking

There are a few elements to this project, the first one we wanted to tackle was ball tracking. Using a raspberry pi, a pi camera, and openCV software, we created a script that can identify colors and create a mask as seen in the below image.

We then create a contour around this mask and store the center value to get the position of the ball. By running this script continuously, we get a live feed of ball position in two dimensions.

As a proof of concept, we then hooked up the pi to a small servo motor and wrote a basic script to track the ball left and right. We defined three areas within the camera, left, right, and center. Based on which region the ball falls into, the corresponding pwm signal is sent to the servo. The video below shows the results, the servo tracks the ball in real time.

Pinball Prototype

We next wanted to make a 2D scaled-down version of this robot. We decided to model it based on a pinball design, with two bumpers to hit tha ball back. By building out a platform, attaching two servo motors to the bumpers and a camera to the top frame looking down, we were able to get a clean design that we presented as part of our capstone progress report.

3D Prototype

The next step was to implement two cameras to capture position data in 3D. The below image shows the two camera system feed as I hold a ball between them. I wrote an algorithm that talkes in the pixel values from each camera, and maps them to a physical 3D position.

We then built two robotic arms made from servos and 3D printed parts. This arms each have two degrees of freedom and thus can move to any location within a 2D plane. By combining the camera position script with an inverse kinemtics script, we were able to get the arms to follow the ball. The next steps are to implement a trajectory program to predict ball position while it follows a parabola.
